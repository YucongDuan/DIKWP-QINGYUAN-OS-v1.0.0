# Architecture

## Runtime object

For item `x`, the runtime constructs:

```text
Q(x) = (D, I, K, W, P, M_e, M_m, M_h, A, R, L)
```

- `D`: source and evidence records;
- `I`: consequential discrepancies, manipulation signals, and model conflict;
- `K`: bounded current assessment;
- `W`: harm, autonomy, expression rights, vulnerable-audience and fairness configuration;
- `P`: named, leased, reversible intervention and evidence-return contract;
- `M_e`: evidence/provenance world model;
- `M_m`: manipulation/addiction/incentive world model;
- `M_h`: harm/autonomy/expression-rights world model;
- `A`: appeal packet;
- `R`: typed residual queue;
- `L`: append-only hash ledger.

The runtime never computes a single truth or moral score. It uses thresholded conditions and rights caps to construct a least-restrictive plan.

## Ten stages

1. contract and authority boundary;
2. lexical/design screening;
3. evidence world model;
4. manipulation world model;
5. harm and rights world model;
6. intervention planning;
7. incentive circuit and real-life support;
8. DIKWP semantic regeneration;
9. residual and appeal registration;
10. bounded certificate.

## Censorship firewall

A public-interest, critical, whistleblowing, minority, quoted, or satirical item receives a suppression-risk signal. Restrictions are capped unless there is a separate high-stakes danger, fraud, or displacement condition. This prevents “information safety” from becoming viewpoint control.

## Active elimination target

The primary elimination targets are not people or lawful ideas. They are:

- opaque amplification boosts;
- undisclosed affiliate and sponsorship incentives;
- compulsive engagement mechanics;
- high-pressure transaction paths;
- evidence-free certainty;
- source/provenance erasure;
- forced isolation and irreversible action.
